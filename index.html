About Me

I'm a PhD candidate at Washington University in St. Louis, where I research how to improve insulin-producing β-cells derived from stem cells — work that could one day transform diabetes treatment. My journey in science started early: at age 14, I began mentoring children newly diagnosed with type 1 diabetes through ADINA in Peru, an experience that shaped my commitment to purpose-driven research.

Beyond the lab, I've served as Senior Director of the DBBS Student Advisory Committee, delivering 30+ programs reaching 1,000+ attendees, and was selected by the Peruvian National Academy of Sciences as a delegate to the Nobel Prize Dialogue. I believe that great science happens when we build inclusive communities and communicate our discoveries with clarity and passion.

“

Research should serve people. Every experiment in my lab connects back to a child I once mentored who was learning to live with diabetes.
